## Teaching and safeguarding

All examples use fictional observations and cannot alter actual IA work or progress. Learning content does not imply a method is compulsory or within an individual student's IB Mathematics course. The existing course caution remains visible.

- A probability tree requires outgoing branch probabilities to total one; conditioned directions and sampling denominators must be checked.
- Expectation is a long-run weighted mean, not a likely individual payoff. Explicitly calculate net outcomes and consider downside and SD.
- Simulations approximate a stated model; additional runs reduce Monte Carlo variability without correcting wrong assumptions. Record the trial, algorithm, random settings and run count.
- Binomial counts require fixed trials, two outcomes, independence and a constant success probability. The exact and cumulative events must match the question.
- A normal curve is a continuous model whose parameters are mean and standard deviation; whether the model fits data must be supported.
- The inference illustration is a **pre-specified one-sided exact binomial test**, not blanket approval to apply a t-test or chi-square test. A p-value is not the probability that the null is true and significance is not practical importance.

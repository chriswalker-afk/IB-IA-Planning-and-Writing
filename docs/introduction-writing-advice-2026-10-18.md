# Introduction Writing Advice and Set up handover — 18 October 2026

## Journey placement

This pass keeps the existing distinction between **C00 — Set up: start writing your IA** and **C10 — Introduction**.

- **C00** now contains the **Set up your IA document** panel. It tells students to create or open their actual Word/Google Docs document, and links directly to **IA essentials → Format and length**. A short, qualified summary says the approximate 12–20 double-spaced pages applies to the *whole IA*, not to its introduction.
- **C10** no longer displays the document-setup panel or its format button. Its purpose is writing a mathematically motivated opening.
- No field transfers, autosave, student mathematics, progress marks, or saved reflections are changed automatically.

## Introduction

The introduction explicitly marks two **Planning sparks** for use in the student's own IA writing:
1. **Mathematical aim** — the answerable mathematical question, not just a theme or topic.
2. **Genuine motivation** — observation, problem or theoretical curiosity. The underlying motivation spark prefers the dedicated motivation field, while still using the existing beneficiary/intended-use fallback if that is all the student saved.

Absent sparks expose a link back to the correct original Planning stage. Saved sparks remain editable in the original shelf; neither opening a spark nor reading advice means it has been incorporated into an actual document.

A six-tip **Writing Guide · Introduction** uses the same style as the Writer's Field Guide. The three prominently listed principles are a precise aim, a genuine motive, and presenting the *start of the mathematical journey rather than an itinerary*. Three optional extra tips cover concise paragraphs, brief definitions of non-mathematical specialist words, and predictions as provisional rather than results. A reduced-advice view retains all three essential instructions while hiding optional comparisons.

## Criteria

Two context-specific read-only badges explain **Criterion A** (coherent, focused, concise introduction; no prescribed word count) and **Criterion C** (genuine mathematical motivation and independent decisions through the full IA). The full official criteria remain available from each badge, with IB descriptor wording unchanged and no inference of achieved marks.

## Example comparisons

- **EX01** (tea modelling), **EX02** (music practice and maths scores), and **EX03** (theoretical integer pattern) now favour motivation, specific mathematical aims and open inquiry instead of listing preselected techniques.
- New **EX34** demonstrates a one-sentence definition of an external specialist term (dough hydration) before returning to the mathematical aim. It is fictional, as are all other teaching examples.
- Each revised introduction example includes three accessible, expandable **What makes the stronger introduction work?** annotations drawing attention to relevant excerpts. These are learning illustrations, not passages to copy.

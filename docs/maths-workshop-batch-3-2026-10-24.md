# Maths Workshop — Batch 3 (24 October 2026)

## Scope
Adds **20 concise definitions** and **five full guided lessons** to the existing Maths Workshop, for **100 definitions and 18 guided lessons** across Batches 0–3.

The lessons cover: quadratic expressions and turning points; exponential growth/decay and logarithms; sinusoidal parameters and periodicity; first and second derivatives for rates and motion; and definite integration as accumulated change.

Each follows **Show me → Help me → My turn**, with a worked mathematical example, two independent guided checkpoints with feedback, independent practice, relevant technology shortcuts, existing tutorial links, and advice on what actually belongs in an IA.

The mathematical emphasis is on **deriving, interpreting and validating** a model. A curve that fits points is not automatically the correct structure for a physical or statistical investigation.

## Curriculum and credibility boundaries
- Examples are fictional and never copied into student investigations.
- Algebraic turning points and optimisation require feasible-domain checks and interpretation of parameters.
- Exponential and sinusoidal patterns must be justified by evidence or assumptions; neither a short time-series nor a visual resemblance establishes a durable model.
- Calculus techniques may exceed some students' selected syllabus; students should check suitability with their teacher.
- Differentiate the **model** to estimate velocity or rate; this is not equivalent to direct physical measurement of that derivative.
- Definite integrals give signed accumulation. Distinguish net displacement from total distance and add initial conditions where appropriate.

## Mappings
- Fitted models → quadratic structure and exponential functions.
- Growth → exponential models and logarithms.
- Cycles → sinusoidal functions.
- Trajectories → quadratic functions and rates of change.
- Optimisation → quadratic optimisation with domain and global-maximum checks.
- Images → integration and signed areas where a justified continuous model supports this.
- Finance → exponential functions when compound growth is part of the model.

Existing regression, descriptive statistics, native plotting and software guides remain available. The routes do not prescribe a method or suggest it automatically proves an IA criterion.
